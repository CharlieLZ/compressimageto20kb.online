Professional upload guide

Job application photo upload: sizes that get reviewed

A recruiter spends seconds on a profile, often on a laptop with a slow portal preview. The photo needs to look like you, load quickly and survive an applicant tracking system. That is a different brief from a passport photo and a different brief from a social media headshot.

Updated 14 November 20267 min readTool runs locally

Quick answer

Use a square headshot between 600×600 and 1200×1200 pixels, saved as JPEG at 100KB to 500KB. If the portal hard-caps at 20KB, follow the cap, but expect softer detail. Keep the face centred, the background plain and the filename simple: firstname-lastname-headshot.jpg.

What the reviewer actually sees

Most applicant tracking systems display a thumbnail beside your name, then a larger preview when the recruiter opens the profile. The thumbnail may be 96×96 pixels; the preview may be 300×300. A 20KB file can look acceptable at 96×96 and visibly soft at 300×300. That is why the byte cap is not the only target: the image must remain clear at the size the reviewer uses.

The same profile may be viewed on a phone during a quick screening and on a large monitor during an interview panel. A mid-sized JPEG covers both. A 4000-pixel camera file wastes portal bandwidth and may hit the upload limit. A 200-pixel thumbnail is too small for the larger view. A 600×600 to 1200×1200 file is the practical middle.

Headshot, portfolio image or document scan?

Job portals often accept several image types, and the right dimensions differ. A headshot is square or portrait and should show the face clearly. A portfolio image may be a landscape project shot where detail matters. A document scan needs legible text and is usually better as PDF, not JPG, unless the portal demands an image.

Why a clean 100KB beats a muddy 20KB

At 600×600, a 20KB file gives about 0.46 bits per pixel. JPEG has to quantise aggressively, and faces develop a waxy, blocky look that reads as a poor photo even when the lighting was fine. At 100KB the same dimensions get about 2.28 bits per pixel, enough to preserve skin texture, hair edges and the contrast around the eyes. If the portal allows 100KB, spending it is almost always the better professional choice.

There are exceptions. Some government-linked job portals impose a strict 20KB cap and will not accept more. In that case, reduce the dimensions as well as the quality: a crisp 400×400 image at 20KB often looks better than a mushy 600×600 one. Check whether the portal also imposes a minimum dimension before shrinking.

Filenames, formats and ATS behaviour

Applicant tracking systems are conservative. Use a lowercase filename with hyphens, no spaces and no special characters: alex-morgan-headshot.jpg. Avoid IMG_4821.JPG, photo final final (2).jpg and emoji. Some systems preserve the filename in recruiter downloads, and a clean name looks more organised.

Use JPEG for photos. PNG is acceptable for logos, screenshots and images with transparency, but it produces larger files for photographic content. Do not rename a PNG to .jpg; the portal may inspect the file signature and reject it, and the bytes will not shrink. Convert through an encoder if JPEG is required.

Composition rules that matter more than compression

  • Face size: the head should occupy a comfortable portion of the frame, not a tiny dot in a wide shot.
  • Background: plain and uncluttered. A bookshelf can be fine; a busy street is not.
  • Lighting: even, with no strong shadow across one side of the face.
  • Expression: neutral or a slight smile. Avoid extreme angles and heavy filters.
  • Clothing: simple and appropriate to the role. Avoid logos and distracting patterns.
  • Crop: square for portals that display a circular avatar; leave headroom so the crop does not cut the forehead.

Privacy when applying online

Job applications spread personal data across many systems. Re-encoding the photo removes EXIF metadata such as GPS coordinates and device details, which is a modest but useful reduction in exposure. It does not remove the visual information in the image. Use a photo you are comfortable seeing in a forwarded PDF, and avoid including your home address or ID number in the image itself.

Keep the original high-resolution photo. If you are invited to an interview or asked for a higher-quality file, you can produce it without re-compressing a compressed copy. The portal copy is a delivery format, not an archive.

When the portal crops your face

Many applicant systems force a square or circular crop in the profile header. If your photo is a tall portrait with the face near the top, the automatic crop can cut the forehead or place the chin at the edge. Preview the image inside a square before uploading. A photo that already has the face centred and enough margin on all sides survives both square and circular crops.

Do not try to defeat the crop with a wide shot where the face is tiny. The thumbnail will become an unreadable dot. Crop closer than feels natural for a social profile; the reviewer needs to recognise you at 96 pixels, not admire the background.

Using one photo across regions and portals

Application norms differ. Some countries expect a photo, others discourage it to reduce bias, and some portals make it optional. Follow the local convention and the form’s instruction. A neutral headshot works in most contexts, but a full suit and studio backdrop can look out of place for a creative or remote role. When the photo is optional and you do not have a current one, leaving it blank is usually better than uploading a low-quality crop.

Keep a master copy at 1200×1200 with modest compression. From that master you can generate a 600×600 version for a strict portal, a 400×400 version for a thumbnail-only field, and a 100KB delivery copy without returning to the camera roll each time.

Frequently asked questions

Should a job photo be square?

Square is the safest default because many portals crop to a circle or square avatar. A portrait crop can work if the preview frame matches, but square avoids awkward automatic cropping.

Can I use the same photo for every application?

Yes, if it is current and neutral. Update it when your appearance changes substantially. Reusing a good photo is better than submitting a rushed, poorly lit new one.

Is 500KB too large for a job portal?

Some portals cap at 100KB or 200KB. Read the stated limit. If there is no limit, 500KB is still small enough to load quickly and gives a clear headshot.
